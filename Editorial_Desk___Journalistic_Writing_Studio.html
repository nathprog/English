House Style

Core Principles of British Analytical Journalism

01. Concrete Verbs Over Weak Nouns

Eliminate nominalizations (nouns ending in -tion, -ment, -ance) whenever a direct verb can carry the action.

Avoid: "The ministry made an announcement regarding the implementation of cuts."

Editorial: "The ministry announced spending cuts."

02. Front-Load the Contrast

Analytical writing thrives on nuance and tension. Use crisp pivot markers—yet, however, still, nonetheless, by contrast, for all X—to turn a sentence cleanly.

Editorial: "The policy looks generous on paper; in practice, few households will qualify."

03. Understatement & Precision

Shun exclamation marks and inflated adjectives (massive, terrible, incredible). Favor diagnostic terms (acute, negligible, protracted, ill-conceived).

Editorial: "The delay proved costly, leaving the regulator exposed to legal challenges."

04. Sentence Cadence

Follow a dense, clause-rich analytical sentence with a short, declarative punch. Contrast in length creates authority and keeps complex arguments readable.

Editorial: "Having spent billions subsidizing legacy factories, ministers expected swift job creation. They were disappointed."

Daily Training Routine

The 30% Cut Drill

Edit a 150-word draft down to 100 words without losing a single fact. Delete adverbs (very, actually) and wordy prepositions.

Syntactic Compression

Merge two or three choppy facts into one fluid sentence using an opening participial phrase or appositive.

Reverse-Engineering

Read an Economist Leader and highlight every sentence-opening transition and main verb. Notice how rarely to be is the main verb.

No-Jargon Translation

Explain a technical financial or legal mechanism in plain, vivid English accessible to an intelligent layperson.
