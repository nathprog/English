    House Style

    Core Principles of British Analytical Journalism

    1. Concrete Verbs Over Weak Nouns (Kill Nominalizations)

    Cut -tion, -ment, and -ance nouns whenever an active verb can carry the sentence.

    Clunky: The government made an announcement regarding the implementation of new tariffs.
    Sharp: The government announced new tariffs.

    2. Front-Load the Contrast (The Pivot Structure)

    Use crisp pivots—yet, however, still, by contrast, for all—to turn an argument cleanly.

    Flat: The policy looks very generous when you read it, although actually almost nobody will qualify.
    Sharp: The policy looks generous on paper; in practice, few will qualify.

    3. Understatement and Diagnostic Precision

    Avoid exclamation marks and inflated adjectives (massive, incredible, disastrous). Favor dry, exact qualifiers: acute, negligible, protracted, ill-conceived.

    4. Vary Cadence for Momentum

    Follow a clause-rich analytical sentence with a short, declarative punch. And watch out for French false friends: translate souligner as underscore, un tournant as a watershed, and malgré tout as for all / even so.
